# Funding and fundraising routes for Blorenge recovery

**Checked:** 31 July 2026  
**Decision question:** Which funding routes are sufficiently relevant and current for the Blorenge recovery proposal workflow to track, and what must that workflow capture?

## Executive finding

There is no first-party announcement of a dedicated Blorenge wildfire-recovery fund. BCA should therefore assemble projects around outcomes that existing programmes already buy: protected-site recovery, landscape-scale habitat resilience, peat and hydrology restoration, commons management, community participation, evidence gathering, and climate adaptation.

The strongest immediate routes are:

1. **Nature Networks Fund (round six)** for a large, partnership-backed SSSI recovery programme. Its expression-of-interest deadline is **12 noon on 5 August 2026**, so BCA should contact the Wales team immediately rather than attempt a speculative full proposal. It can fund planning, surveys, training, staffing, community participation and physical restoration from **£250,000 to £1 million**, with no mandatory match funding.
2. **Ffermio Bro** as the best-fit agricultural route, despite its 2026 expression-of-interest window being closed and new submissions going to a waiting list. Its Bannau Brycheiniog programme expressly names registered commons, upland habitat and peatland, GPS livestock collars and firebreaks.
3. **Rolling community and heritage programmes** for smaller evidence, participation, digital and recovery projects: National Lottery Heritage Grants, National Lottery Awards for All Wales, People and Places, Small Eco Awards, and the Bannau Brycheiniog nature-recovery microgrants.
4. **Crowdfunder** as the best initial external public-fundraising host if BCA is an unincorporated not-for-profit association; **JustGiving Crowdfunding** is a credible simpler alternative. The BCA site should link out and reconcile results, not process payments.

The first gating fact is **BCA's legal form, governing document, board composition, bank account name and accounts**. A constituted association is eligible for several public/community programmes, but Aviva's Communities Fund and Big Give exclude it unless BCA becomes an eligible incorporated body or registered charity. Incorporation or charity registration should be a deliberate governance decision, not an accidental consequence of a funding application.

## Status vocabulary

- **Open** means a first-party page said applications were being accepted on the checked date.
- **Recurring** means a repeat/annual mechanism exists but its current intake has closed.
- **Expected** means the programme or policy continues, but no next application window has been announced. It is a watch item, not an available grant.
- **Closed** means the cited round is over and should not appear to users as actionable.

The platform must store the source-backed status and `lastCheckedAt`, because a programme name can persist while its rules, amount, geography and window change.

## Open routes

### 1. Nature Networks Fund (round six) — urgent, highest strategic fit

**Status and window.** Open for mandatory expressions of interest until **12 noon, 5 August 2026**. Invited full applications close **12 noon, 5 November 2026**; decisions are expected by the end of February 2027. Awards are **£250,000–£1 million**. The Blorenge SSSI is within the fund's protected-site definition, and the scheme funds action on protected sites and their ecological networks, including planning, surveys, training and community participation. There is no mandatory cash or non-cash match contribution. [Round-six programme guidance](https://www.heritagefund.org.uk/funding/nature-networks-fund-round-six)

**Fit and eligibility.** This is the clearest vehicle for a coordinated post-fire ecological baseline and multi-year restoration programme, not a small website build. The lead must be an organisation working with natural heritage in Wales and can lead only one application. The proposal must name the protected site/features, demonstrate an evidence-based ecological rationale, align with site management/conservation objectives, include Welsh throughout, show public benefit over private gain, and have or be working toward land permissions, licences and consents. A strong route would make BCA the local-knowledge and commoners' delivery partner within a credible ecological/landowner partnership; lead-applicant capacity must be tested with the Heritage Fund.

**Application and evidence.** Online EOI followed by invitation-only online application. Mandatory supporting material includes governing document, three years of audited/verified accounts (or permitted alternatives for a newer body), current management accounts, project plan and risk register, capital/revenue cost template, land ownership documents, regional and works maps identifying protected features, management/change-control structure, and post-project risk plan. Partnership agreement, commissioned-work briefs, job descriptions and full-cost-recovery calculation become mandatory where applicable. File limit is 20MB.

**Decision and payments.** EOI response is targeted within 20 working days. Full applications are assessed for protected-network condition/connectivity, equitable access, organisational capacity, sustained impact, geographic/biodiversity spread, value/risk and evidence. Grants are paid **in arrears** in instalments against expenditure evidence, so cash flow belongs in the proposal model.

**Reporting and data reuse.** A final evaluation is required before the final 10% payment, including ecosystem resilience/connectivity benefit, monitoring data and method. Geospatial/work-location data must be shared to enhance NRW/Welsh Government datasets; species records go to a local environmental records centre and/or public database. Digital outputs are expected under **CC BY 4.0** by default. Procurement over £10,000 requires at least three competitive tenders/quotes; stronger evidence is required over £50,000.

**Automation.** Submission is through the Heritage Fund online service. No public application-submission API is documented. The BCA product should generate an EOI/application pack and deep-link to the portal; a human must submit and attest it.

**Immediate action.** Email `wales@heritagefund.org.uk` and `sitesactions@cyfoethnaturiolcymru.gov.uk` before the EOI deadline with a one-page recovery concept, Blorenge SSSI reference, indicative partnership and proposed planning/delivery split. Do not imply that a rushed EOI guarantees invitation or funding.

### 2. National Lottery Heritage Grants — rolling natural and digital heritage route

**Status and amount.** Open and rolling in Wales. Grants are **£10,000–£250,000** with no deadline and an eight-week decision target after a complete application; grants over **£250,000–£10 million** require an EOI and, if invited, use periodic committee deadlines (the next listed deadline after the checked date is 6 August 2026, then 12 November 2026). Natural habitats, species, landscapes and community engagement can be heritage. [£10,000–£250,000 guidance](https://www.heritagefund.org.uk/funding/national-lottery-heritage-grants-10k-250k-0), [over-£250,000 deadlines](https://www.heritagefund.org.uk/funding/national-lottery-heritage-grants-250k-10m/deadlines)

**Fit and eligibility.** Strong for a smaller package joining fire-damage evidence, local knowledge, biodiversity monitoring, interpretation, public participation and the bilingual digital portal. It is less tightly restricted to protected-site capital delivery than Nature Networks. Cash contributions are encouraged but not universally mandatory. Land rights, permissions and public benefit remain important.

**Application and evidence.** Online application with explicit word-limited questions on vision, heritage and risk, need, community, access/inclusion, organisational sustainability/capability, project management, outcomes, budget, risks, permissions, digital works and subsidy control. Project plan, risk register, accounts and relevant consents/condition evidence are required. The current question set is published and should be represented as a versioned template, not scraped into permanent hard-coded fields. [Current application questions](https://www.heritagefund.org.uk/funding/national-lottery-heritage-grants-10k-250k/application-questions-0)

**Reporting and data reuse.** Evaluation is mandatory. Grant-funded original digital outputs are normally **CC BY 4.0**; code and metadata are **CC0** or an accepted open-software licence. Public digital availability is required for five years for awards up to £250,000 and 20 years above that; websites must meet at least W3C A/AA respectively. Species/habitat records should be shared with NBN Atlas and/or a local environmental records centre. These rules directly affect contributor consent, media rights, dataset licensing, accessibility and long-term hosting design. [Digital requirements](https://www.heritagefund.org.uk/funding/good-practice-guidance/digital)

**Automation.** Online portal; no public submission API. Generate a downloadable application pack and keep portal submission manual.

### 3. National Lottery Awards for All Wales — small community launch projects

**Status and amount.** Open, rolling, **£300–£20,000**, for projects up to two years; decision target 12 weeks. A constituted voluntary/community organisation or club can apply, with at least two unrelated directors/committee members. The programme will consider community places/spaces and organisations adapting to challenges. [Programme](https://www.tnlcommunityfund.org.uk/funding/funding-programmes/national-lottery-awards-for-all-wales), [eligibility](https://www.tnlcommunityfund.org.uk/funding/funding-programmes/national-lottery-awards-for-all-wales/who-can-apply)

**Fit.** Good for an early bilingual member/public evidence-capture pilot, volunteer survey training, community events or basic field equipment when framed as community benefit and participation. It is not a substitute for ecological capital restoration funding. The maximum from this programme is £20,000 in any 12 months; no concurrent application/current award is allowed for the same programme.

**Application, decision and reporting.** A saveable online form is reviewed within about 12 weeks; successful awards are targeted for payment within 14 days. The fund may request more information and check progress. It requires bilingual Welsh delivery and adherence to award terms/publicity. [How to apply](https://www.tnlcommunityfund.org.uk/funding/funding-programmes/national-lottery-awards-for-all-wales/how-to-apply)

**Data reuse and automation.** No project-output open-licence mandate or public application API was identified in the current programme guidance. Submission remains human through the online form.

### 4. People and Places — medium community platform/recovery programme

**Status and amount.** Open, **£20,001–£500,000**, up to five years. Medium grants (£20,001–£100,000) use a one-stage process and target about 15 weeks; large grants (£100,001–£500,000) use two stages and take at least 40 weeks. [Programme](https://www.tnlcommunityfund.org.uk/funding/funding-programmes/people-and-places)

**Fit and eligibility.** Potentially strong for the community participation, access, local-knowledge, volunteering and long-term stewardship parts of the recovery platform. At least 80% of beneficiaries must live in Wales. Constituted community groups are eligible, with at least three unrelated committee members. The application must show community co-design, existing local provision/gaps, strengths, inclusion, environmental impact and bilingual delivery. [Eligibility](https://www.tnlcommunityfund.org.uk/funding/funding-programmes/people-and-places/who-can-apply), [funding expectations](https://www.tnlcommunityfund.org.uk/funding/funding-programmes/people-and-places/what-we-want-to-fund)

**Application, reporting and automation.** Online medium/large application processes; payment plan, progress updates and assurance are agreed at award. No public application API or general open-output licence was identified. Treat the form and award/report terms as versioned attachments.

### 5. Small Eco Awards — small environmental action

**Status and amount.** Newly open from July 2026 in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, **£300–£20,000**, with a 12-week decision target and up to 24 months to spend. It funds community-led work whose main aim is environmental sustainability: connecting people with nature, improving the environment, pollution reduction and adaptation to challenges. [Programme page](https://www.tnlcommunityfund.org.uk/funding/funding-programmes/small-eco-awards)

**Fit.** Strong candidate for a bounded volunteer monitoring, habitat-care or post-fire community action package. Current detailed eligibility/application/reporting sub-guidance was not exposed on the checked programme page, so the workflow must require a live eligibility check before recommending it.

**Automation.** National Lottery Community Fund application account/portal; no public submission API identified.

### 6. Sustainable Steps Wales — Egin Grants

**Status and amount.** Open, advertised at **£100–£15,000** on the detailed programme page (some fund-list pages display £35,000, so the detailed page must control until the fund confirms otherwise). Applicants must first receive support from the Egin mentoring programme. It can fund capital and revenue costs including equipment, project staff, volunteers, translation, learning and evaluation. [Detailed programme](https://www.tnlcommunityfund.org.uk/funding/programmes/sustainable-steps-wales-egin-grants), [eligible costs](https://www.tnlcommunityfund.org.uk/funding/funding-programmes/sustainable-steps-wales-egin-grants/what-you-can-spend-the-money-on)

**Fit.** Possible for community climate adaptation/learning and bilingual evidence activity, but only after Egin mentoring and when climate engagement is the main aim. Land/building work requires ownership/lease, permissions and three quotes.

**Reporting/data.** Award terms require restricted-fund accounting, dual financial control, access to records/systems for inspection, acknowledgement and equal Welsh/English engagement; Big Lottery may publicise project information and images under its stated licence. [Current terms](https://www.tnlcommunityfund.org.uk/our-policies/programme-terms-and-conditions/sustainable-steps-wales-egin-grants-standard-terms-and-conditions/)

### 7. Bannau Brycheiniog community/landowner nature-recovery grants

**Status and amount.** The National Park page says a second funding round runs until March 2027 and invites enquiries. It offers up to **£5,000** under “Communities Working for Nature Recovery” and “Landowners Working for Nature.” [National Park grant page](https://beacons-npa.gov.uk/communities/help-for-communities-and-groups/)

**Fit.** Useful for small, practical pilots such as habitat enhancement, invasive-species work, woodland/hedgerow work or natural flood measures. If an applicant has an RPW Customer Reference Number, the Park says Ffermio Bro should be the first route. The public page does not publish a complete live form, decision timetable or reporting terms; BCA should contact the Park and store the resulting guidance as a dated source.

**Automation.** Enquiry-led/manual; no API.

### 8. Woodland Creation Planning Scheme and Woodland Creation Grant — conditional only

**Status.** The Planning Scheme is open throughout 2026 and offers **£1,000–£5,000** for a verified woodland creation plan. An approved plan remains eligible to apply to the Woodland Creation Grant for five years. Woodland Creation Grant window 6 is open until **20 November 2026**. [Planning Scheme rules](https://www.gov.wales/woodland-creation-planning-scheme-january-2026-rules-booklet-html), [rural application dates](https://www.gov.wales/rural-schemes-application-dates)

**Fit and constraints.** This is not a presumption that burned upland/SSSI habitat should become woodland. Use only where ecological assessment supports appropriate riparian, gully, boundary or agroforestry planting. Common land requires Commons Act section 38 consent or written confirmation that it is unnecessary; designated-site planting needs consent from relevant bodies including NRW. A registered woodland planner, verified GIS plan/shapefile, land registered with RPW, management control and consultation are required. The small-grants planting route is **not** suitable for registered common land or priority habitat.

**Application/reporting/automation.** RPW Online EOI and Woodland Plan Register, with official plan templates and shapefiles; claims follow NRW verification and require invoices/bank evidence for surveys. There is no public submission API. The BCA platform may export GIS and evidence but must not try to impersonate RPW Online.

### 9. Landfill Disposals Tax Communities Scheme — one conditional window remains

**Status and amount.** Standard **£5,000–£49,999** windows closed on 5 June and 17 July 2026. The **£50,000–£250,000 nationally significant grant** remains open until **11:59pm, 21 August 2026**; only one may be awarded annually and it must contribute to at least two themes. Projects must normally be within five miles of an eligible waste facility; biodiversity activity outside the radius can be considered case by case. **Blorenge eligibility has not been established** and must be checked on the round-specific map with WCVA. [Live scheme page](https://wcva.cymru/funding/landfill-disposals-tax-communities-scheme/)

**Eligibility and evidence.** A constituted organisation with governing documents and organisational bank account can apply. Required evidence includes accounts, environmental/equality policies, permissions/land agreements and any match confirmation. Community benefit and support must be evidenced; alterations/capital work normally need a lease/licence or durable landowner assurance.

**Application, decision and reporting.** Apply through WCVA's Multipurpose Application Portal. WCVA eligibility review, County Voluntary Council assessment, quality assurance and advisory panel lead to a decision within 12 weeks. Funding is 80% in advance and 20% after the final report; six-month monitoring reports and target/milestone measures are required. [2026 applicant guidance](https://wcva.cymru/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/LDTCS-Guidance-for-Applicants.pdf)

**Data/automation.** No open-output mandate or application API is documented. Portal submission is manual.

### 10. Aviva Foundation Communities Fund — rolling crowdfunding match, legal-form gate

**Status and amount.** Open year-round while the annual pot remains, with **up to £25,000 match funding per organisation per calendar year** across at most two projects. Each public donation is matched up to £250 through Crowdfunder. Climate action expressly includes nature restoration, biodiversity, water quality, green space and community engagement. [Fund page](https://communitiesfund.avivafoundation.org.uk/)

**Eligibility.** Annual income no more than £1m; UK beneficiaries and organisation bank account; at least 12 months' operation. Only a registered charity, CIO, CIC or company limited by guarantee can apply. **Constituted community groups are expressly ineligible**, making BCA's legal form decisive. [Eligibility criteria](https://communitiesfund.avivafoundation.org.uk/eligibility-criteria)

**Workflow.** Online application, identity/bank due diligence, then a Crowdfunder campaign. Public-donation fees apply but match donations are fee-free. Submission and attestation are manual; campaign progress can be reconciled using Crowdfunder CSV reports.

## Recurring or expected routes that are not open now

### Ffermio Bro — recurring programme; 2026 intake closed/wait-list

Ffermio Bro runs in designated landscapes until March 2028. The Bannau Year 2 EOI window opened 16 March 2026 but closed 22 May because of demand; later applications go onto a waiting list. Farms must be wholly or partly in the National Park. Awards are **£1,001–£20,000** per farm CRN per year and are capital grants completed within the financial year. Crucially, published Year 2 examples include registered-commons projects, upland habitat and peatland improvement, bracken/scrub control, boundaries, GPS livestock collars and **firebreaks**, along with River Usk natural flood management and water-quality work. The programme seeks multi-farm/land-manager collaboration, with individual grants to each applicant. [Bannau Ffermio Bro page](https://beacons-npa.gov.uk/environment/ffermio-bro/)

Eligibility mirrors the Sustainable Farming Scheme Universal Layer (normally at least 3ha eligible agricultural land including common apportionment, or the labour-hours alternative, RPW registration, management control and agricultural activity). Application development is adviser-led through the National Park, not a BCA-built submission API. Avoid double funding and preserve CRNs, parcel IDs, collaborators, standard intervention rates, quotes, consents, delivery-year and claim evidence in the proposal model.

**Action:** contact the eastern Park adviser now for the wait-list and for advice on whether a common-wide recovery package should be split among graziers or coordinated through the association.

### Sustainable Farming Scheme Universal/Collaborative support — annual; 2026 SAF closed

The 2026 Single Application Form ran 2 March–15 May. Common-land graziers can receive Universal payments if they hold grazing rights, can turn out suitable livestock, maintain a stocking diary for inspection and have regard to common management. The scheme description anticipates collaborative payments to Grazing Associations for coordinated grazing and enhanced management. Optional/Collaborative mechanisms and rates are still being developed/published. [SFS 2026 scheme description](https://www.gov.wales/sustainable-farming-scheme-2026-scheme-description-html)

This is farmer/rights-holder support rather than a BCA project grant. The platform should help members preserve the underlying livestock, grazing, habitat and intervention evidence, but never represent that it has submitted an SAF/RPW claim.

### National Peatland Action Programme restoration grant — expected repeat, no open round

NRW lists the **£10,000–£250,000** Peatland Restoration Grant as closed on 14 January 2026. The last round offered 100% funding for development and/or delivery to private landowners and public/voluntary organisations, with a £700,000 pot and delivery by March 2027. NRW's 2025–2030 peatland plan aims to scale restoration and specifically links it to flood and wildfire resilience; NRW says other funding mechanisms may also be discussed. [Last round](https://naturalresources.wales/about-us/news-and-blogs/news/2025-news-releases/more-please-for-peatland-restoration-grant/?lang=en), [current grant status](https://naturalresources.wales/about-us/grants-and-funding/current-grant-funding-opportunities/?lang=en)

Do not display a next deadline until NRW publishes one. First establish whether mapped/burned Blorenge areas are peat and obtain hydrology/habitat evidence; then contact `npap@naturalresourceswales.gov.uk` about direct mechanisms and future rounds.

### Integrated Natural Resources Scheme — programme delivery continues; no new intake announced

The original INRS development application closed in September 2024 and its delivery-plan deadline was March 2025. Current 2026 guidance shows funded projects in delivery, while the Nature Recovery Action Plan says the programme will continue as an Optional/Collaborative SFS mechanism. It supports landscape-scale partnerships among farmers, foresters, graziers, land managers and agencies, with delivery grants of **£250,000–£1 million** in the prior round. [INRS rules](https://www.gov.wales/integrated-natural-resources-scheme-rules-booklet-html), [programme collection](https://www.gov.wales/integrated-natural-resources-scheme), [2026 Nature Recovery Action Plan](https://www.gov.wales/nature-recovery-action-plan-2026-html)

This is a watch/partner route, not an open application. Preserve partnership governance, landscape geometry, four-or-more target outcomes, interventions, permissions, indicators, value for money, delivery sustainability and RPW identifiers so a future call can be assessed quickly.

### Big Give Earth Raise and Christmas Challenge — annual match campaigns; 2026 applications closed

Earth Raise (formerly Green Match Fund) is the strongest environmental match campaign, but 2026 applications closed 16 January. Christmas Challenge 2026 applications closed 3 July. Both are only for UK-registered/tax-exempt charities; most campaigns require at least £25,000 annual income. Earth Raise supports environment as a core charitable mission and offered £1,000–£10,000 public-donation targets under 1:1 matching or larger pledge-model campaigns. [Earth Raise](https://biggive.org/earth-raise/), [2026 calendar](https://biggive.org/blog/2025/10/14/2026-campaign-calender/)

Track future annual calls only if BCA becomes a charity or uses a charity lead. From 1 July 2026 Big Give's card processing charge is **1.9% + 25p per donation, plus VAT**, with no platform fee; match-campaign funds are restricted to the approved application until target conditions are met. [Fee update](https://biggive.org/blog/2026/04/16/fee-changes-statement/)

## External public-fundraising providers

The BCA website should own the proposal narrative, evidence and approvals, but send donors to a provider-hosted checkout/campaign page. Store only the external campaign URL/ID, public totals, reconciliation records and consented supporter data exported by the provider. Never collect card data.

### Recommended default: Crowdfunder UK

- Supports not-for-profit community projects, social enterprises and charities; creator must be 18+, use a UK address and qualifying UK bank account.
- Not-for-profit community projects have 0% platform fee and **2.4% + 20p plus VAT per UK/EEA pledge**; charity profiles use **1.9% + 20p plus VAT**. Non-UK cards cost more. Match funds can attract 0–5% plus VAT depending on partner. [Current fees](https://www.crowdfunder.co.uk/fees)
- Supports Keep What You Raise and All or Nothing (charity/personal fundraisers default to flexible funding), payout schedules, team roles, widgets and potential partner match funding. [Funding models](https://help.crowdfunder.co.uk/en/articles/1642526-what-s-the-difference-between-all-or-nothing-and-flexible-funding), [team permissions](https://help.crowdfunder.co.uk/en/articles/6679990-adding-users-and-managing-permissions-on-your-project)
- Transaction, payout, Gift Aid, rewards and marketing-consent reports are available as password-protected CSV downloads. [Reports](https://help.crowdfunder.co.uk/en/articles/1779392-how-do-i-download-my-supporters-details)
- No documented public campaign-management API was found. Use outbound links/widgets and manual CSV import; do not scrape dashboards. Public totals could be recorded manually unless Crowdfunder grants specific integration access.

**Why preferred initially:** UK community focus, match-funding ecosystem, 0% platform fee for a constituted not-for-profit, organisation roles and usable CSV reconciliation. Verify the exact BCA bank/legal-name setup before launch.

### Simpler alternative: JustGiving Crowdfunding

- Crowdfunding supports personal causes, clubs, schools and communities. It has 0% platform fee and **2.9% + 35p per GBP card donation**; withdrawal to a UK bank account is by BACS. [Crowdfunding product](https://www.justgiving.com/for-crowdfunding), [current fees](https://www.justgiving.com/about/fees)
- JustGiving also has a charity product: free Start membership, 1.9% + 30p processing, optional 5% charge on Gift Aid reclaimed; paid Grow plans add reporting/integrations. [Charity pricing](https://www.justgiving.com/for-charities/pricing)
- A documented REST API can create/read fundraising pages and donations with an AppID, and a separate Data API plan exists. However, current documentation does not establish that ordinary **Crowdfunding** campaigns receive the same write/data access; charity integrations are associated with the paid plan. Verify access with JustGiving before designing an adapter. [Developer documentation](https://developer.justgiving.com/apidocs/documentation)

**When to choose:** a very simple public appeal where platform recognition matters more than match-funding/organisation workflow, or if its support team confirms useful Crowdfunding API access.

### Fallback: GoFundMe

- UK individual/business fundraisers have no platform fee and **2.9% + 25p** per donation; funds can go to an organisation bank account if an authorised financial administrator completes verification. Certified charity campaigns use PayPal Giving Fund and cost 1.9% + 20p. [UK pricing](https://www.gofundme.com/en-gb/c/pricing), [organisation transfers](https://support.gofundme.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360040924571-Choosing-a-transfer-type)
- GoFundMe Pro has an API, but that is a separate nonprofit product; no public API for an ordinary GoFundMe campaign was identified. [GoFundMe Pro API](https://developers.gofundme.com/pro/)

**Why not first choice:** fewer UK community-match features than Crowdfunder and no verified ordinary-campaign API benefit.

### Big Give

Use only after charity/tax-exempt eligibility is established and for a scheduled match campaign. It offers excellent leverage but is not an always-available substitute for Crowdfunder/JustGiving. The 2026 environmental and Christmas application windows are closed.

## Product decision: what the proposal workflow must do

### Opportunity record

Each opportunity should be a dated, source-backed record with:

- canonical name, funder/administrator, first-party URL, contact and source document/version;
- status (`open`, `recurring_closed`, `expected`, `closed`, `unknown`), status evidence, checked date, opening/closing times and timezone, recurrence and next-review date;
- geography and boundary test, including a saved eligibility-map result where relevant;
- eligible applicant legal forms, minimum age/trading history/income, committee/board rules, bank/account requirements, partnership/lead rules and prior-award exclusions;
- land-management control, ownership/lease duration, common-land/SSSI/planning permissions and public-access requirements;
- grant minimum/maximum, project length, match funding, VAT, cash-flow/payment timing, retrospective-cost rule and double-funding restrictions;
- funded themes/costs, exclusions and BCA fit rationale;
- application stages, form/template version, word/character/file limits, portal/deep link, decision body/timescale and earliest project start;
- required accounts, policies, maps, quotes, partnership/support letters, ecological baseline and consents;
- monitoring indicators, claim evidence, reporting cadence, evaluation, audit/record-retention, publicity and clawback;
- digital-output/data-sharing requirements, licence, accessibility, Welsh-language and publication duration;
- automation classification (`none`, `export_only`, `documented_read_api`, `documented_write_api`, `partner_api`) with evidence and scope;
- uncertainty/eligibility questions and named owner for resolving them.

### Reusable project/proposal record

Keep funder-neutral facts once, then map them into versioned funder questions:

- problem/need, fire chronology and evidence provenance;
- authoritative and proposed geometries, designations, habitats/features and site objectives;

- chair/secretary approval and immutable submitted-version snapshot.

### Application behaviour

1. **Discover and assess, do not auto-submit.** Generate a human-reviewable pack (Markdown/PDF/DOCX, budgets and GIS exports), show current portal instructions, then require the authorised person to submit externally.
2. **Status must expire.** An open opportunity becomes `unknown` after its review-by date until a moderator rechecks the first-party source. Never infer a new annual deadline from last year's date.
3. **Preserve a submission snapshot.** Store every exported answer, attachment checksum, source opportunity version, approval and external receipt/reference.
4. **Separate public from private.** Publish approved project narrative, totals and outcomes; keep accounts, bank data, land agreements, personal supporter data, draft budgets and portal credentials private.
5. **Make licensing deliberate.** Contributor evidence must record whether it may remain private, be published, and be released under the funder's required open licence. Personal data is never made open merely because related project data is.
6. **Support manual reconciliation first.** External campaign URL plus CSV import is enough for the first fundraising integration. Add an API adapter only when the provider documents the exact account/product permissions and the operational value exceeds maintenance cost.
7. **Track obligations after award.** Deadlines, claims, procurement, indicators, reports, publicity and licence/publication terms are part of the project lifecycle, not notes discarded after submission.

## Newly exposed decisions

1. **What is BCA's exact legal and financial identity?** Constitution, incorporation/charity status, bank name, annual income, accounts age and number/independence of committee members determine eligibility.
2. **Should BCA lead the large protected-site recovery programme or be the local/commoners partner to a higher-capacity lead?** Nature Networks requires organisational, ecological, financial and cash-flow capacity well beyond a small digital project.
3. **Is peat restoration relevant on the burned Blorenge area?** The answer needs mapped peat depth/condition and hydrological assessment, not assumption from the upland setting.
4. **Who owns or controls each proposed works area and can durable third-party agreements/Commons Act and SSSI consents be obtained?** This gates almost every capital route.
5. **What is the first fundable vertical slice?** A community evidence/monitoring pilot can start sooner than the full ecological programme and has different funders.
6. **Should BCA change legal form to unlock charity/incorporated-only funding and Gift Aid?** This is a governance/liability/tax decision, not merely a fundraising feature.
7. **Which external fundraising provider becomes the first supported integration?** Crowdfunder is the evidence-based default, but an account-level proof-of-concept should verify reports, roles and BCA bank onboarding before locking it in.
